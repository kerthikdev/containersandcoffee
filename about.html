The Story

I got into DevOps the same way most people do — I was a developer who got tired of saying "it works on my machine" and decided to do something about it. That was the beginning of a rabbit hole that led me through Dockerfiles, Helm charts, Terraform modules, and three different Kubernetes clusters (two of which I deliberately destroyed as learning exercises).

The coffee obsession started around the same time. There's something about the precision required for a good espresso — the variables, the feedback loops, the constant iteration — that resonates deeply with how I think about infrastructure. Both reward patience, attention to detail, and a willingness to throw out what isn't working and start again.

This blog is where I document what I learn. It's part technical reference for my future self, part attempt to explain complex concepts in a way that actually makes sense, and part love letter to the intersection of craft and technology.

If you're a DevOps engineer, a developer who wants to understand infrastructure better, or just someone who appreciates a good cup of coffee and a clean deployment pipeline — you're in the right place.

The Philosophy

Why Containers & Coffee?

🎯

Precision matters in both

A few degrees of water temperature changes a coffee's flavor profile entirely. A misconfigured resource limit can take down a production cluster. Both disciplines reward careful attention to detail.

🔄

Iteration is the path to mastery

Your first Dockerfile will be bloated. Your first espresso will be sour. Neither of these is failure — they're just pull requests that need another round of review.

🧘

The ritual grounds the work

Making coffee deliberately — measuring, timing, attending — is a form of mindfulness. It's the mental transition between "asleep" and "ready to debug a Kubernetes networking issue." Don't skip the ritual.
